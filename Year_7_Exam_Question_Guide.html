Copy or explain the meaning?

1. Find a word or phrase

Find one word meaning “tiny”.

Show how to answer
Answer format

QUOTE: one exact word or phrase.

What to do

Copy from the given lines. A full sentence is not needed.

Answer pattern

“[word from the text]”

Watch out

One word means one word. A two-word phrase must have two words.

2. Explain a word’s meaning

Explain what “scarce” means here.

Show how to answer
Answer format

OWN WORDS: a short phrase or sentence.

What to do

Give a meaning that fits the sentence. For example: not enough.

Watch out

“Find a word meaning…” asks you to copy. “Explain the meaning…” asks you to explain. An antonym is an opposite.

3. Give two words or phrases

Give two phrases showing that Ella is worried.

Show how to answer
Answer format

QUOTES: two separate bullet points.

What to do

Copy two short pieces of proof. No explanation is needed here.

Answer pattern

• “[first phrase]”
• “[second phrase]”

Watch out

Use different evidence. If asked for adjectives, copy adjectives.

Reasons, opinions and proof

4. Explain a reason

Why does Ella refuse the invitation?

Show how to answer
Answer format

OWN WORDS: a short full sentence.

What to do

Explain the reason. Use what the text tells or suggests.

Answer pattern

She refuses because [reason].

Watch out

Explain why, not just what happened. For “surprising”, show what we expected and what happened instead.

5. Give a view and prove it

Do you think Ella feels welcome? Support your answer with a quotation.

Show how to answer
Answer format

BOTH: your reason + an exact quote.

What to do

Give your view and a reason. Then copy words that prove it.

Answer pattern

Yes / No, because [reason].
Quotation: “[matching evidence]”

Watch out

“Yes” alone is not enough. A quote alone does not explain your view. Use Reason / Quotation spaces if given.

6. Give two supported points

Give two ways the guide helps visitors. Support each with a quotation.

Show how to answer
Answer format

BOTH: two separate reason-and-quote pairs.

Answer pattern

1. [Reason]. Quote: “[proof]”.
2. [Different reason]. Quote: “[proof]”.

Watch out

For this 4-mark pattern: two points + two matching quotes. Do not repeat a point.

The writer’s choices and ideas

7. Explain a choice or an effect

How does the writer create a sense of speed?

Show how to answer
Answer format

OWN WORDS: a brief explanation.

What to do

Name a relevant choice and say how it helps. If asked only what is emphasised, name the feeling or idea.

Answer pattern

[The choice] makes [this effect].

Watch out

“It is interesting” is too vague. For punctuation, explain its job here. Add a quote if the question asks for one.

8. Compare grammar or structure

Explain how the first sentence differs in structure from the others.

Show how to answer
Answer format

OWN WORDS: two clear parts.

Answer pattern

The first sentence [feature].
The other sentences [different feature].

Watch out

Use grammar terms when asked about grammar. Copying an opening word does not name its word class. Compare both sides.

9. Find a link or a contrast

Which idea from the opening returns at the end?

Show how to answer
Answer format

OWN WORDS: one clear idea.

What to do

Read both parts. State the idea they share.

Answer pattern

Both parts return to [shared idea].

Watch out

For “What contrasting idea does Yet introduce?”, state the change: first [idea], but then [opposite idea].

Follow the answer format

10. Choose the correct answer

Tick two boxes.

Show how to answer
Answer format

TICKS: exactly the number requested.

What to do

Read every option before choosing.

Watch out

No written explanation is needed unless asked for.

11. Complete a fact file

Complete the fact file using the text.

Show how to answer
Answer format

TABLE: a short factual entry in each space.

What to do

Names, words and phrases are enough.

Watch out

Complete every row. Six facts can earn three marks.

12. Summarise the main ideas

Summarise the main ideas. Use no more than 50 words.

Show how to answer
Answer format

OWN WORDS: one short paragraph.

What to do

Start with a topic sentence: say what the text is mainly about. Use connectives to list and link the main ideas.

Example

The article explains how a community garden helps local people. It also shows how residents share the work. In addition, the garden gives neighbours a place to meet.

Connectives

Useful links: Also, In addition, Finally. Write a comma after In addition or Finally at the start of a sentence.

Watch out

No dates or extra details. Use full sentences, not bullets. Check capitals, commas, full stops and the word limit.
